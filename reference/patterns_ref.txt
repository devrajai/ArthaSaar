Chart Patterns — reference (ArthaSaar)
Last checked: 2026-10-17. Educational reference, not investment advice.
A pattern is a shape in price that has historically leaned one way more often than not. It is a probability, not a promise — the same shape fails regularly. Use it to frame a trade (entry, stop, target), never as the whole reason for one.
__________________________________________________
1. Reversal patterns (trend is ending)
Pattern	Shape	Bias	How to read it
Head & Shoulders	Three peaks, middle highest	Bearish	Neckline = lows between the peaks. Break below it confirms. Target = peak height projected down.
Inverse Head & Shoulders	Three troughs, middle lowest	Bullish	Mirror of the above; break above the neckline.
Double Top	Two similar highs, trough between	Bearish	Confirms on a break of the trough. Volume often lighter on the 2nd peak.
Double Bottom	Two similar lows, peak between	Bullish	Confirms on a break of the middle peak.
Triple Top / Bottom	Three touches of a level	Reversal	More touches = more decisive, but also a slower break.
Rounding Top / Bottom	Smooth saucer shape	Reversal	Gradual shift in momentum; slow to form, slow to confirm.
Rising / Falling Wedge	Converging, sloped	Bearish / Bullish	Rising wedge in an uptrend warns of a stall; falling wedge in a downtrend of a bounce.
2. Continuation patterns (trend pauses, then resumes)
Pattern	Shape	Bias	How to read it
Bull / Bear Flag	Sharp move, then tight counter-drift	Continue	The pole (sharp move) projects the target from the breakout.
Pennant	Small symmetrical triangle after a move	Continue	Shorter than a flag; breakout in the trend direction.
Ascending Triangle	Flat top, rising lows	Bullish	Buyers stepping up; break of the flat top.
Descending Triangle	Flat bottom, falling highs	Bearish	Break of the flat bottom.
Symmetrical Triangle	Both sides converging	Either	Wait for the break — direction is undecided until then.
Cup & Handle	Rounded base + small dip	Bullish	Handle should be shallow; break of the handle high.
Rectangle / Range	Flat top and bottom	Either	Trade the edges, or wait for the range break.
3. Candlestick patterns (single / few bars)
Pattern	Shape	Bias
Doji	Open ≈ close, long wicks	Indecision
Hammer	Small body, long lower wick	Bullish reversal
Shooting Star	Small body, long upper wick	Bearish reversal
Bullish Engulfing	Green body covers prior red	Bullish
Bearish Engulfing	Red body covers prior green	Bearish
Morning / Evening Star	Three-bar reversal	Bullish / Bearish
Marubozu	Full body, no wicks	Strong conviction

4. Reading them from data (numeric checks)
Patterns become useful when you can detect them on the archive rather than by eye. Rough starting rules, computed per symbol on data/candles/cm_daily.parquet:
Pattern	Numeric hint
Double Top	Two local highs within ~1% of each other, separated by a trough ≥ ~3%
Head & Shoulders	Three local highs where the middle > both sides by ≥ ~2%, shoulders within ~2%
Flag	Price move ≥ ~5% in ≤ 10 bars, then a counter-drift with range < ~half the pole
Triangle	Highs falling and lows rising (converging) over ≥ 15 bars
Cup & Handle	Drawdown ≥ ~15% then recovery to near the old high, then a ≤ ~5% dip
Hammer	lower_wick ≥ 2 x body and upper_wick small
Engulfing	today's body fully covers yesterday's body, opposite colour
Local highs/lows: a bar whose high is the max of the surrounding N bars (and vice versa for lows). N ≈ 5 is a reasonable default.
Always confirm with volume and the trend. A double top inside a strong uptrend is noise; the same shape after a long run is information.

5. Discipline (the part that actually decides the outcome)
● Pattern is a setup, not a signal. Wait for the confirmation break; a pattern that never breaks is not a trade.
● Define the stop before the entry — usually the other side of the pattern (neckline, flag low, handle low).
● Position size from the stop distance, not from conviction.
● Backtest the rule, not the story. Detect the pattern numerically, measure the hit rate on your own archive, and only then trust it.
● Most patterns fail. The edge, if any, is in the risk management and the confirmation, not the shape.
